# Content from IATE on téarma.ie

## What is IATE?

IATE (**I**nter**a**ctive **T**erminology for **E**urope) is the EU's terminology management system. It has been used in the EU institutions and agencies since summer 2004 for the collection, dissemination and management of the terminology dealt with by the EU institutions and bodies. For more information go to [iate.europa.eu/about](https://iate.europa.eu/about).

## The Irish language and IATE

When Irish became an official working language of the EU in 2007, there was a massive increase in the volume of documents that had to be provided in the language. That responsibility fell on the translation units within the various EU institutions. One of the challenges facing translators at the time was the lack of Irish entries in IATE. Among the support measures established by the Irish Government at the time was the LEX project, an initiative to increase the number of Irish entries in IATE. The contract to run the project was awarded to Gaois, DCU, which operated it in close cooperation with the Irish-language units in the EU institutions and the Terminology Committee (An Coiste Téarmaíochta) from 2008 to 2024. The Irish units in the institutions were growing and expanding during this time and they gradually built capacity to carry out more terminology work internally. Those institutions are now entirely responsible for the Irish language content in IATE. IATE currently contains around 90,000 entries containing Irish-language data and they are all now accessible through www.tearma.ie.

## How are these two terminology collections related?

First of all, what is found under the 'téarma.ie' tab (and as the default search) are results from the Terminology Committee's database. These are terms that have been published over the years in the Committee's publications and, for the past two decades, on the www.téarma.ie website. The Terminology Committee and the Téarma team are responsible for this terminology, and it is updated and edited on an ongoing basis.

Secondly, what is found under the IATE tab is a synchronized version of the entries in IATE, the European Union's terminology database, that contain Irish content. In each case, an abbreviated copy of the original IATE entry is provided, offering comprehensive information in Irish and English along with basic information (the term only) for 5 other languages (French, German, Italian, Spanish, Latin). Although this is a live copy of what is in IATE, the Téarma team has no control over or responsibility for the content found within it.

## Overlap between Téarma and IATE

There is significant overlap between the two sources. The same concept is often found in both, using the exact same Irish and English terms. See, for example, [test.tearma.ie/iate/generative AI/](https://test.tearma.ie/iate/generative%20AI/).

That being said, there are also plenty of examples where the same concept exists in both collections, but the Irish or English terms do not completely align e.g. [test.tearma.ie/q/parasite/](https://test.tearma.ie/q/parasite/). However, such a discrepancy does not mean that one source is 'right' and the other 'wrong'. While errors can exist in either source, these differences often relate to the distinct goals of the two databases. IATE terminology is rooted in EU legislation, where adherence to specific words, phrasing, and titles is required for consistency. Similarly, the terminology stored in the National Terminology Database has long-standing ties to the Irish education system, with many terms originally created for a textbook or an exam paper. Consistency is extremely important in that context as well.

If you have a query about a discrepancy between the two sources, you can send it to <tearma@dcu.ie>, but please note that 1. the Téarma team does not have editorial access to IATE terminology, and 2. there is no intention to align or harmonize these two terminology sources.
